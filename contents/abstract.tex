%% 中文摘要页
\begin{ChineseAbstract}
云原生应用广泛依赖 Kubernetes 生态中的第三方组件。受过度授权、访问控制缺陷、敏感信息泄露和网络隔离不足等因素影响，权限提升漏洞在披露到补丁完成发布和部署之间往往存在较长窗口期，给生产环境带来持续风险。本文对 2020—2025 年间 55 个云原生权限提升漏洞样本进行统计分析，结果表明，30.4\% 的漏洞在披露后存在较长攻击窗口。现有方法多侧重通用静态核查、配置审计或单项检测，难以针对具体漏洞生成可快速部署、业务干扰较小的临时防控方案，且策略制定过程较为依赖专家经验，制约了方法的复用与推广。

针对上述问题，本文提出一种面向云原生权限提升漏洞补丁窗口期的智能防控方法。本文构建了云原生权限提升漏洞分类框架，并从代码仓库、漏洞公告、Issue 和 Pull Request 等材料中提取相关证据，建立安全知识库。在此基础上，将防控措施划分为监控策略和防护策略两类，设计结合知识检索、分步推理和反馈优化的协同生成流程，实现从漏洞分析到策略生成的自动化。同时，围绕有效性、无干扰性和可部署性三个方面建立候选策略排序评价方法，并通过多评审聚合提高结果稳定性。为验证方法的工程可行性，本文设计并实现了原型系统 KPEAgent。

本文在 55 个云原生权限提升漏洞样本上开展实验研究。结果表明，反馈优化机制对策略质量提升作用最为显著，移除该机制后策略有效性下降 79.2\%；知识检索和分步推理同样能够带来稳定增益，三者协同作用下完整方法在有效性、无干扰性和可部署性三个维度上均取得最优表现。与基线方法相比，本文方法生成策略的有效性提升 32.5\%，可部署性提升 41.2\%；多评审聚合得到的排序结果较单一评审稳定性提升 23.7\%，且与人工评审结果整体一致。案例分析表明，生成的策略能够在准入控制、权限收敛、网络隔离和运行时监测等环节快速落地，有效压制补丁窗口期内的攻击路径并降低风险暴露面。研究结果可为云原生环境下权限提升漏洞的临时防控提供方法依据和工程参考。

\ChineseKeywords{云原生安全；权限提升漏洞；补丁窗口期防控；多智能体协同；策略生成与评估}
\end{ChineseAbstract}

%% 英文摘要页
\begin{EnglishAbstract}
Cloud-native applications rely heavily on third-party components in the Kubernetes ecosystem. Excessive privileges, access-control flaws, sensitive information exposure, and insufficient network isolation often leave privilege escalation vulnerabilities exploitable during the interval between disclosure and patch deployment, thereby posing persistent risks to production environments. An analysis of 55 cloud-native privilege escalation vulnerabilities collected from 2020 to 2025 shows that 30.4\% of them have a prolonged attack window after disclosure. Existing approaches mainly focus on generic static inspection, configuration auditing, or isolated detection, and therefore cannot easily provide temporary mitigation strategies that are tailored to specific vulnerabilities, rapidly deployable, and minimally disruptive to business services. In addition, the formulation of such strategies still depends heavily on expert experience, which limits reuse and practical deployment.

To address these issues, this thesis proposes an intelligent prevention-and-control method for privilege escalation vulnerabilities during the patch-gap stage in cloud-native environments. A classification framework for cloud-native privilege escalation vulnerabilities is first constructed, and a security knowledge base is established by extracting relevant evidence from code repositories, vulnerability advisories, issues, and pull requests. On this basis, mitigation measures are divided into monitoring strategies and protection strategies, and a collaborative generation workflow integrating knowledge retrieval, step-by-step reasoning, and feedback optimization is designed to automate the process from vulnerability analysis to strategy generation. In addition, a ranking-based evaluation method is introduced from the perspectives of effectiveness, non-interference, and deployability, and multi-judge aggregation is adopted to improve the stability of the evaluation results. To further verify engineering feasibility, a prototype system named KPEAgent is designed and implemented.

Experiments are conducted on 55 cloud-native privilege escalation vulnerability samples. The results show that feedback optimization contributes most significantly to policy quality, and removing this mechanism reduces effectiveness by 79.2\%. Knowledge retrieval and step-by-step reasoning also provide stable gains, and the complete method achieves the best overall performance in effectiveness, non-interference, and deployability. Compared with baseline methods, the proposed method improves effectiveness by 32.5\% and deployability by 41.2\%. Moreover, the ranking results produced by multi-judge aggregation are 23.7\% more stable than those of a single judge and are broadly consistent with human assessment results. Case studies further show that the generated strategies can be rapidly deployed in admission control, privilege reduction, network isolation, and runtime monitoring, thereby suppressing attack paths and reducing risk exposure during the patch-gap stage.

\EnglishKeywords{Cloud-native security; Privilege escalation; Patch-gap defense; Multi-agent collaboration; Policy generation and evaluation}


\end{EnglishAbstract}
